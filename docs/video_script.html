3. Ready-to-Speak Judge Q&A Defense (Simple & Confident)

Here are the top questions judges frequently ask, along with easy, conversational answers you can speak effortlessly:

Q1: "What if a genuine early-stage startup emails a candidate from a normal Gmail address? Will you mark them as a scam?"

"Great question, judges! No, we won't. In VeriHire, using Gmail only adds fifteen penalty points. To be marked as a suspicious offer, a document needs thirty points, and to be marked as a scam, it needs sixty-five points. A real startup offer doesn't demand advance money, doesn't steal original certificates, and doesn't use fake company names. So a legitimate startup offer stays safely in the green zone."

Q2: "What if a scammer rewrites their letter using ChatGPT so the grammar is 100% perfect?"

"Even if the scammer polishes their grammar with AI, they still have to ask the candidate for money or documents. More importantly, their sender email domain will still be fake, their document metadata will lack enterprise HR system stamps, and their HR signatory will not exist in corporate registries. Scammers cannot fake all six layers at the same time."

Q3: "Why did you build an on-device engine instead of using a cloud AI API like OpenAI or Claude?"

"For three crucial reasons: speed, privacy, and cost. On-device analysis finishes in under five milliseconds, whereas cloud APIs take three to five seconds. For privacy, job offers contain sensitive student IDs and home addresses, which cannot legally be sent to third-party cloud servers under the DPDP Act. And for cost, our solution runs completely free forever on standard hardware."

Q4: "How does VeriHire make money as a business?"

"We use a B2B SaaS model. For individual students, the basic scan is always free. Universities pay an annual institutional license for our Placement Clearance Portal to verify batch offers. In addition, recruitment platforms like LinkedIn, Indeed, and Naukri can license our headless API to pre-screen recruiter postings before they go live."

Q5: "How does MailGuard AI protect user credentials when connecting to Gmail?"

"MailGuard uses Google's official sixteen-character App Passwords, never the user's primary Google account password. This App Password grants access only to the email protocol, can be revoked by the user in one click from their Google security dashboard, and is never transmitted to any external server."
